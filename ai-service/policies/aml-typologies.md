# AML Typologies

## Structuring
Structuring means splitting cash deposits into several amounts just below a reporting limit to avoid attention. Typical signs are repeated deposits within a few days, amounts close to the limit, and deposits across several branches. Analysts should review the pattern as a whole and not each deposit alone.

## Layering and circular flows
Layering moves funds through several accounts to hide their origin. A circular flow returns money to the starting account after passing through intermediaries. Look for transfers between a small group of accounts with little commercial reason, similar amounts at each step and short gaps between transfers.

## Rapid movement of funds
A burst of many transactions in a short period, or funds that leave an account soon after arriving, can indicate that the account is used as a pass through. Compare the pattern with the customer's normal activity before concluding anything.

## Dormant account reactivation
An account with no activity for a long time that suddenly receives or sends a large amount deserves review. Confirm that the customer is in control of the account and ask about the purpose of the funds.

## Income and activity mismatch
Turnover that is much larger than declared income or the expected volume given at onboarding is a warning sign. Ask the customer to explain and update the risk rating if the explanation is weak.

## Large single transactions
A single transaction above the internal threshold is flagged for review. Many large transactions are legitimate, so the decision depends on the customer profile and supporting documents.
